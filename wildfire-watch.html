Actually rained at your house?
This gauge is a simplified estimate — wind, humidity, heat, and days since significant rain (≥0.1in/2.5mm, from Open-Meteo's daily rainfall history) — computed live and rebalanced across those four factors. It is not an official fire danger rating. For the authoritative rating, check the Great Basin GACC and Utah Fire Info links below. Always defer to official Red Flag Warnings and evacuation orders.

Local note: Trailside/Sagebrook sits directly against Round Valley's sagebrush, scrub oak and grassland — a classic wildland-urban interface. That fuel dries out fast in a hot, low-humidity stretch, so treat sustained wind + low humidity + a long rain-free stretch as your cue to check FIRMS even with no smoke visible yet.

Smoke and particulates often arrive before the fire itself — sometimes from hundreds of miles away. A sudden AQI spike with no visible local fire is often the first sign of a new fire upwind of you. Check the AQI widget above alongside these metrics and cross-reference with NASA FIRMS.

Should You Leave Even Without an Official Alert?

Official alerts fail more often than people expect. After the 2023 Lahaina fire, a survivor survey found most people's first warning wasn't an official alert at all — about half saw the fire themselves, and many more were tipped off by neighbors already leaving. Cell towers, sirens, and dispatch centers have all failed at critical moments in fires like Camp, Tubbs, and Eaton/Palisades. Don't wait on a notification that may never arrive — check these honestly.
